# Students ask, the teacher answers yes or no

Teachers found "letting a student go" hard to follow: from the laptop they gave
one of three Permissions ahead of time (an Extra Pass, a No-Pass Exception or a
Line Skip), then the student had to tap their name at the kiosk to use it, and
the button lived on each class's page. Now a blocked student sends a Request
from the kiosk, and the teacher answers it on Home, or enters their PIN at the
kiosk. Whatever is blocking the student, the teacher's choice is the same, and
a yes starts the pass at once. We chose one question with a yes or no answer
over separate permissions because teachers think "can this student go?", not
"which rule should I lift?".

## Consequences

- The three Permission kinds are no longer things a teacher hands out. They
  survive as labels on the pass (Extra Pass, No-Pass Exception, Line Skip), so
  the Pass Allowance still counts correctly and History shows which rule an
  approval lifted.
- Like the Line, Requests belong to the kiosk; the laptop only answers them.
  A student can ask only while the kiosk reaches the laptop. Otherwise the PIN
  is the only way past a block.
- A yes starts the pass even if the student has sat back down. We accepted a
  slightly early start time over a second tap, which teachers found confusing.
- Denied Requests are kept in History alongside passes, so a teacher can show
  who asked and was told no. Requests nobody answered leave nothing.
- There is no way to let a student go before they ask.
